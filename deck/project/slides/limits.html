Anomalies and caveats

A few stores stand out, and the data has limits we must fix

Stores worth a closer look

  • Philadelphia (event): +58% during, +32% after, the best lasting result
  • New York (discount): +67% from the lowest baseline, $15 per sq ft
  • Los Angeles (BOGO): 14% below baseline afterwards, the worst hangover
  • Chicago (event): visits stayed 29% up, but sales did not; spend per visit fell to about $12.70
  • El Paso, San Antonio: busiest stores, lift of only 5–8%

Why these are signals, not proof

  • Every store promoted, so there is no untreated control group
  • Each store ran one type, so type is tangled with location and size
  • Dates span April to August, so seasonality is mixed in
  • Before and after windows are not defined; promotions ran 14 or 15 days
  • No cost, discount depth or margin, so we can’t judge profit

Source: GreenTrail Store Data

Two things to flag. On the left, the stores worth a conversation with the regional teams: Philadelphia is our best lasting result and worth copying; Los Angeles had the worst post-promotion drop; Chicago brought people back but they spent less. On the right, the honest caveats. Every store promoted, so we have no true control. Each store ran only one type. Dates were spread across the season. And we have no margin data, so I can’t tell you which promotion made the most money. That’s exactly why the next step is a controlled test, not a rollout.
