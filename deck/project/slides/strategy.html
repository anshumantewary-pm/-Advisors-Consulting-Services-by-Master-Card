Proposed strategy

Four moves that reward durable choices, not extra consumption

Stop

Retire Buy-One-Get-One

Weakest lift (+30%), the only negative after-effect (−2%), and it pushes customers to buy more than they need.

Lead · Test arm A

Trail Community Days

Repair clinics, Leave No Trace workshops, trail clean-ups with local parks groups, gear swaps. Events kept 25% of their lift with no price cut.

Add · Test arm B

Event + ReGear credit

Store credit for trading in used gear or for a repair, spent on durable or recycled lines within 30 days. Drives a second visit and shifts spend to the eco range.

Target

Spend where there’s room

Prioritise stores under about $23 of weekly sales per sq ft, and urban stores. Busy stores such as El Paso get low-cost events and loyalty perks, not price cuts.

Any remaining discounts: shallow, on sustainable lines and end-of-season stock only

So what do we do? Four moves. Stop BOGO: it performs worst and conflicts with our values. Lead with community events, which I’m calling Trail Community Days: repair clinics, skills workshops, clean-ups, gear swaps. These had the best lasting effect and cost us no margin on price. Add a ReGear credit: trade in old gear or get something repaired, and get credit to spend on our durable and recycled lines within 30 days. That gives people a reason to come back and moves them toward the products we want to sell. And target the money where stores have spare capacity. Where we still use discounts, keep them shallow and on sustainable lines or end-of-season stock that would otherwise be wasted.
